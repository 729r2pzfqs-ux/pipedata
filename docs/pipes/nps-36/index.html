What NPS 36 is

NPS 36 pipe measures 36.000 in (914.4 mm) across the outside, so here the size designation and the outside diameter in inches are the same number. That holds from NPS 14 upward and for no size below it. The metric designator is DN 900, which is likewise a label and not a measurement in millimetres. Whatever the schedule, the outside diameter stays at 36.000 in (914.4 mm): a heavier wall grows inward and takes the difference out of the bore.

The largest size tabulated on this site. B36.10M continues well beyond it. It is typically supplied as welded pipe, with flanges to ASME B16.47.

Reading the table

B36.10M lists 6 designations in NPS 36, which between them give 5 different wall thicknesses. The lightest is Schedule 10 at 0.312 in (7.9 mm) and the heaviest is Schedule 40 at 0.750 in (19.0 mm). Going from one to the other takes the bore from 35.376 in (898.6 mm) down to 34.500 in (876.3 mm), which cuts the flow area by 5% and raises the weight from 118.92 to 282.36 lb/ft, a factor of 2.4.

Standard weight does not coincide with any numbered schedule in this size. Extra strong equals Schedule 20. Where two designations share a wall they are the same pipe under two names, and either may appear on a mill certificate. The match is specific to the size, so it is the wall thickness in inches that should go on a requisition, not the designation alone.

For handling and support loads, a 20 ft length of standard weight NPS 36 pipe weighs about 2854 lb (1294 kg) empty and 11315 lb (5133 kg) full of water. The water-filled figure is the one that governs pipe support spacing and the load during a hydrostatic test, including on lines that will carry gas or vapour in service.

Flow and pressure capacity by schedule

Two questions decide a schedule: how much the bore will carry, and how much pressure the wall will hold. The flow columns give the water flow through each bore at three velocities that bracket ordinary liquid service, from a pump suction line at the low end to a pump discharge line at the high end. The pressure columns are the ASME B31.3 pressure design equation solved for pressure, first on the nominal wall and then on the wall after the 12.5% mill under-tolerance that seamless pipe is allowed.

Flanges for NPS 36 pipe

ASME B16.5 stops at NPS 24, so there is no B16.5 flange in this size. Flanges for NPS 36 pipe come from ASME B16.47, which publishes two series, A and B, that do not bolt to each other. The series has to be settled before anything else about the flange is. See large diameter flanges.

Common questions

What is the outside diameter of NPS 36 pipe?

NPS 36 pipe has an outside diameter of 36.000 in (914.4 mm). That outside diameter is fixed: it is identical in every schedule, and only the wall thickness — and therefore the bore — changes from one schedule to the next.

What is the inside diameter of NPS 36 schedule 40 pipe?

34.500 in (876.3 mm). The 0.750 in (19.0 mm) wall comes off the 36.000 in (914.4 mm) outside diameter twice, once on each side, which leaves that bore and a flow area of 934.82 in².

How much does NPS 36 schedule 40 pipe weigh?

282.36 lb/ft (420.19 kg/m) as plain-end carbon steel, on a wall thickness of 0.750 in (19.0 mm). It holds 48.562 US gallons of water per foot.

What is the metric equivalent of NPS 36?

DN 900. DN is a dimensionless designator, not a measurement — DN 900 pipe does not measure 900 mm anywhere. Its actual outside diameter is 914.4 mm.
