What NPS 3/8 is

NPS 3/8 pipe measures 0.675 in (17.1 mm) across the outside, which is 0.300 in more than the 3/8 in its name suggests. Below NPS 14 the designation is a label inherited from iron pipe sizing and matches neither the outside diameter nor the bore of any schedule exactly. The metric designator is DN 10, which is likewise a label and not a measurement in millimetres. Whatever the schedule, the outside diameter stays at 0.675 in (17.1 mm): a heavier wall grows inward and takes the difference out of the bore.

The least used of the three sizes below NPS 1/2. Many piping specifications leave it off their size list altogether and step from NPS 1/4 straight to NPS 1/2, so check that fittings and valves are available before designing around it.

Reading the table

B36.10M lists 6 designations in NPS 3/8, which between them give 4 different wall thicknesses. The lightest is Schedule 5 at 0.049 in (1.2 mm) and the heaviest is Schedule 80 at 0.126 in (3.2 mm). Going from one to the other takes the bore from 0.577 in (14.7 mm) down to 0.423 in (10.7 mm), which cuts the flow area by 46% and raises the weight from 0.33 to 0.74 lb/ft, a factor of 2.3.

Standard weight equals Schedule 40 in this size. Extra strong equals Schedule 80. Where two designations share a wall they are the same pipe under two names, and either may appear on a mill certificate. The match is specific to the size, so it is the wall thickness in inches that should go on a requisition, not the designation alone.

For handling and support loads, a 20 ft length of standard weight NPS 3/8 pipe weighs about 11 lb (5 kg) empty and 13 lb (6 kg) full of water. The water-filled figure is the one that governs pipe support spacing and the load during a hydrostatic test, including on lines that will carry gas or vapour in service.

Flow and pressure capacity by schedule

Two questions decide a schedule: how much the bore will carry, and how much pressure the wall will hold. The flow columns give the water flow through each bore at three velocities that bracket ordinary liquid service, from a pump suction line at the low end to a pump discharge line at the high end. The pressure columns are the ASME B31.3 pressure design equation solved for pressure, first on the nominal wall and then on the wall after the 12.5% mill under-tolerance that seamless pipe is allowed.

Flanges for NPS 3/8 pipe

ASME B16.5 publishes no flange in NPS 3/8 and ASME B16.9 no buttweld fitting. That is the practical meaning of an obsolete size: the pipe dimensions are still in B36.10M, but nothing standard is made to connect to it. New work uses the next size up or down.

Common questions

What is the outside diameter of NPS 3/8 pipe?

NPS 3/8 pipe has an outside diameter of 0.675 in (17.1 mm). That outside diameter is fixed: it is identical in every schedule, and only the wall thickness — and therefore the bore — changes from one schedule to the next.

What is the inside diameter of NPS 3/8 schedule 40 pipe?

0.493 in (12.5 mm). The 0.091 in (2.3 mm) wall comes off the 0.675 in (17.1 mm) outside diameter twice, once on each side, which leaves that bore and a flow area of 0.19 in².

How much does NPS 3/8 schedule 40 pipe weigh?

0.57 lb/ft (0.84 kg/m) as plain-end carbon steel, on a wall thickness of 0.091 in (2.3 mm). It holds 0.010 US gallons of water per foot.

What is the metric equivalent of NPS 3/8?

DN 10. DN is a dimensionless designator, not a measurement — DN 10 pipe does not measure 10 mm anywhere. Its actual outside diameter is 17.1 mm.
