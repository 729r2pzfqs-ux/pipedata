Where schedule 30 is used

Schedule 30 exists only in the larger sizes and sits between Schedule 20 and Schedule 40. It is used for large-diameter, low-pressure lines where Schedule 20 leaves too little margin.

How the wall changes with size

The wall in schedule 30 is 0.057 in (1.4 mm) at NPS 1/8 and 0.625 in (15.9 mm) at NPS 36. Measured against the pipe it belongs to, that is 14.1% of the outside diameter at the small end and 1.7% at the large end, so the wall does not keep pace with the diameter. The pressure the pipe will hold moves the same way. On the basis used throughout this site (ASME B31.3, S = 20,000 psi, nominal wall, no corrosion allowance) schedule 30 holds about 6,344 psig at NPS 1/8 and about 704 psig at NPS 36. A schedule is therefore chosen size by size, from a calculation, and not once for a whole plant.

From NPS 28 up to NPS 36 the wall is the same in every size, 0.625 in (15.9 mm). Over that range schedule 30 is a fixed thickness and no longer a series that climbs with the size.

In this schedule the wall thickness coincides with STD in NPS 14 through NPS 16 and XS in NPS 20 through NPS 22. Outside those sizes it does not, so never substitute one designation for the other without checking the number.

Common questions

What sizes is schedule 30 pipe made in?

ASME B36.10M publishes schedule 30 in 27 sizes, from NPS 1/8 through NPS 36. Wall thickness runs from 0.057 in (1.4 mm) at the small end to 0.625 in (15.9 mm) at the large end.

Does schedule 30 mean the same wall thickness in every size?

No. It is a series, not a thickness. The wall is 0.057 in (1.4 mm) at NPS 1/8 and 0.625 in (15.9 mm) at NPS 36, so the thickness always has to be read off the table for the size in hand.
