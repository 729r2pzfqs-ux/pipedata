What NPS 24 is

NPS 24 pipe measures 24.000 in (609.6 mm) across the outside, so here the size designation and the outside diameter in inches are the same number. That holds from NPS 14 upward and for no size below it. The metric designator is DN 600, which is likewise a label and not a measurement in millimetres. Whatever the schedule, the outside diameter stays at 24.000 in (609.6 mm): a heavier wall grows inward and takes the difference out of the bore.

The largest size covered by ASME B16.5 flanges and by the B16.9 tables on this site. Used for cooling water mains, flare headers and large transfer lines.

Reading the table

B36.10M lists 13 designations in NPS 24, which between them give 12 different wall thicknesses. The lightest is Schedule 5 at 0.218 in (5.5 mm) and the heaviest is Schedule 160 at 2.344 in (59.5 mm). Going from one to the other takes the bore from 23.564 in (598.5 mm) down to 19.312 in (490.5 mm), which cuts the flow area by 33% and raises the weight from 55.37 to 542.14 lb/ft, a factor of 9.8.

Standard weight equals Schedule 20 in this size. Extra strong does not coincide with a numbered schedule here. Where two designations share a wall they are the same pipe under two names, and either may appear on a mill certificate. The match is specific to the size, so it is the wall thickness in inches that should go on a requisition, not the designation alone.

For handling and support loads, a 20 ft length of standard weight NPS 24 pipe weighs about 1892 lb (858 kg) empty and 5574 lb (2528 kg) full of water. The water-filled figure is the one that governs pipe support spacing and the load during a hydrostatic test, including on lines that will carry gas or vapour in service.

Flow and pressure capacity by schedule

Two questions decide a schedule: how much the bore will carry, and how much pressure the wall will hold. The flow columns give the water flow through each bore at three velocities that bracket ordinary liquid service, from a pump suction line at the low end to a pump discharge line at the high end. The pressure columns are the ASME B31.3 pressure design equation solved for pressure, first on the nominal wall and then on the wall after the 12.5% mill under-tolerance that seamless pipe is allowed.

Flanges for NPS 24 pipe

ASME B16.5 sizes a flange from the NPS and the pressure class, never from the schedule, so every flange below fits every wall in the table above. In NPS 24 a Class 150 flange is 32.00 in (812.8 mm) across and 1.81 in (46.0 mm) thick, held by 20 bolts of 1 1/4 in. A Class 1500 flange for the same pipe is 46.00 in (1168.4 mm) across and 8.00 in (203.2 mm) thick, on 16 bolts of 3 1/2 in. Because the bolt circle moves with the class, flanges of two different classes will not bolt together even though both fit the pipe.

A weld neck flange for standard weight NPS 24 pipe is bored 23.250 in (590.5 mm); ordered for Schedule 160 it is bored 19.312 in (490.5 mm). The schedule therefore belongs on every weld neck flange requisition.

Buttweld fittings for NPS 24 pipe

ASME B16.9 sets the dimensions of a buttweld fitting from the NPS alone, so the figures below hold for every schedule in the size. A long radius 90° elbow in NPS 24 measures 36.00 in (914.4 mm) from its centre to each end, which is the figure a piping layout adds for every change of direction. The fitting is still ordered to a schedule, because its wall has to match the pipe at the weld.

Common questions

What is the outside diameter of NPS 24 pipe?

NPS 24 pipe has an outside diameter of 24.000 in (609.6 mm). That outside diameter is fixed: it is identical in every schedule, and only the wall thickness — and therefore the bore — changes from one schedule to the next.

What is the inside diameter of NPS 24 schedule 40 pipe?

22.624 in (574.6 mm). The 0.688 in (17.5 mm) wall comes off the 24.000 in (609.6 mm) outside diameter twice, once on each side, which leaves that bore and a flow area of 402.00 in².

How much does NPS 24 schedule 40 pipe weigh?

171.30 lb/ft (254.92 kg/m) as plain-end carbon steel, on a wall thickness of 0.688 in (17.5 mm). It holds 20.883 US gallons of water per foot.

What is the metric equivalent of NPS 24?

DN 600. DN is a dimensionless designator, not a measurement — DN 600 pipe does not measure 600 mm anywhere. Its actual outside diameter is 609.6 mm.
