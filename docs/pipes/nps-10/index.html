What NPS 10 is

NPS 10 pipe measures 10.750 in (273.1 mm) across the outside, which is 0.750 in more than the 10 in its name suggests. Below NPS 14 the designation is a label inherited from iron pipe sizing and matches neither the outside diameter nor the bore of any schedule exactly. The metric designator is DN 250, which is likewise a label and not a measurement in millimetres. Whatever the schedule, the outside diameter stays at 10.750 in (273.1 mm): a heavier wall grows inward and takes the difference out of the bore.

A common size for main process lines, cooling water and flare sub-headers.

Reading the table

B36.10M lists 14 designations in NPS 10, which between them give 11 different wall thicknesses. The lightest is Schedule 5 at 0.134 in (3.4 mm) and the heaviest is Schedule 160 at 1.125 in (28.6 mm). Going from one to the other takes the bore from 10.482 in (266.2 mm) down to 8.500 in (215.9 mm), which cuts the flow area by 34% and raises the weight from 15.19 to 115.65 lb/ft, a factor of 7.6.

Standard weight equals Schedule 40 in this size. Extra strong equals Schedule 60. Where two designations share a wall they are the same pipe under two names, and either may appear on a mill certificate. The match is specific to the size, so it is the wall thickness in inches that should go on a requisition, not the designation alone.

For handling and support loads, a 20 ft length of standard weight NPS 10 pipe weighs about 810 lb (367 kg) empty and 1493 lb (677 kg) full of water. The water-filled figure is the one that governs pipe support spacing and the load during a hydrostatic test, including on lines that will carry gas or vapour in service.

Flow and pressure capacity by schedule

Two questions decide a schedule: how much the bore will carry, and how much pressure the wall will hold. The flow columns give the water flow through each bore at three velocities that bracket ordinary liquid service, from a pump suction line at the low end to a pump discharge line at the high end. The pressure columns are the ASME B31.3 pressure design equation solved for pressure, first on the nominal wall and then on the wall after the 12.5% mill under-tolerance that seamless pipe is allowed.

Flanges for NPS 10 pipe

ASME B16.5 sizes a flange from the NPS and the pressure class, never from the schedule, so every flange below fits every wall in the table above. In NPS 10 a Class 150 flange is 16.00 in (406.4 mm) across and 1.19 in (30.2 mm) thick, held by 12 bolts of 7/8 in. A Class 2500 flange for the same pipe is 26.50 in (673.1 mm) across and 6.50 in (165.1 mm) thick, on 12 bolts of 2 1/2 in. Because the bolt circle moves with the class, flanges of two different classes will not bolt together even though both fit the pipe.

The one flange dimension that does follow the schedule is the bore of a weld neck flange, which is machined to the inside diameter of the pipe it is welded to. A weld neck flange for standard weight NPS 10 pipe is bored 10.020 in (254.5 mm); ordered for Schedule 160 it is bored 8.500 in (215.9 mm). The schedule therefore belongs on every weld neck flange requisition.

Buttweld fittings for NPS 10 pipe

ASME B16.9 sets the dimensions of a buttweld fitting from the NPS alone, so the figures below hold for every schedule in the size. A long radius 90° elbow in NPS 10 measures 15.00 in (381.0 mm) from its centre to each end, which is the figure a piping layout adds for every change of direction. The fitting is still ordered to a schedule, because its wall has to match the pipe at the weld.

Common questions

What is the outside diameter of NPS 10 pipe?

NPS 10 pipe has an outside diameter of 10.750 in (273.1 mm). That outside diameter is fixed: it is identical in every schedule, and only the wall thickness — and therefore the bore — changes from one schedule to the next.

What is the inside diameter of NPS 10 schedule 40 pipe?

10.020 in (254.5 mm). The 0.365 in (9.3 mm) wall comes off the 10.750 in (273.1 mm) outside diameter twice, once on each side, which leaves that bore and a flow area of 78.85 in².

How much does NPS 10 schedule 40 pipe weigh?

40.48 lb/ft (60.25 kg/m) as plain-end carbon steel, on a wall thickness of 0.365 in (9.3 mm). It holds 4.096 US gallons of water per foot.

What is the metric equivalent of NPS 10?

DN 250. DN is a dimensionless designator, not a measurement — DN 250 pipe does not measure 250 mm anywhere. Its actual outside diameter is 273.1 mm.
