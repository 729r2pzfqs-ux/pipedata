What NPS 1/8 is

NPS 1/8 pipe measures 0.405 in (10.3 mm) across the outside, which is 0.280 in more than the 1/8 in its name suggests. Below NPS 14 the designation is a label inherited from iron pipe sizing and matches neither the outside diameter nor the bore of any schedule exactly. The metric designator is DN 6, which is likewise a label and not a measurement in millimetres. Whatever the schedule, the outside diameter stays at 0.405 in (10.3 mm): a heavier wall grows inward and takes the difference out of the bore.

This is the smallest size ASME B36.10M publishes. It turns up on gauge and instrument connections, lubrication lines and sample points rather than on process lines, and it is normally threaded: there is too little wall to butt-weld comfortably.

Reading the table

B36.10M lists 6 designations in NPS 1/8, which between them give 4 different wall thicknesses. The lightest is Schedule 5 at 0.035 in (0.9 mm) and the heaviest is Schedule 80 at 0.095 in (2.4 mm). Going from one to the other takes the bore from 0.335 in (8.5 mm) down to 0.215 in (5.5 mm), which cuts the flow area by 59% and raises the weight from 0.14 to 0.31 lb/ft, a factor of 2.3.

Standard weight equals Schedule 40 in this size. Extra strong equals Schedule 80. Where two designations share a wall they are the same pipe under two names, and either may appear on a mill certificate. The match is specific to the size, so it is the wall thickness in inches that should go on a requisition, not the designation alone.

For handling and support loads, a 20 ft length of standard weight NPS 1/8 pipe weighs about 5 lb (2 kg) empty and 5 lb (2 kg) full of water. The water-filled figure is the one that governs pipe support spacing and the load during a hydrostatic test, including on lines that will carry gas or vapour in service.

Flow and pressure capacity by schedule

Two questions decide a schedule: how much the bore will carry, and how much pressure the wall will hold. The flow columns give the water flow through each bore at three velocities that bracket ordinary liquid service, from a pump suction line at the low end to a pump discharge line at the high end. The pressure columns are the ASME B31.3 pressure design equation solved for pressure, first on the nominal wall and then on the wall after the 12.5% mill under-tolerance that seamless pipe is allowed.

Flanges for NPS 1/8 pipe

ASME B16.5 publishes no flange in NPS 1/8 and ASME B16.9 no buttweld fitting. That is the practical meaning of an obsolete size: the pipe dimensions are still in B36.10M, but nothing standard is made to connect to it. New work uses the next size up or down.

Common questions

What is the outside diameter of NPS 1/8 pipe?

NPS 1/8 pipe has an outside diameter of 0.405 in (10.3 mm). That outside diameter is fixed: it is identical in every schedule, and only the wall thickness — and therefore the bore — changes from one schedule to the next.

What is the inside diameter of NPS 1/8 schedule 40 pipe?

0.269 in (6.8 mm). The 0.068 in (1.7 mm) wall comes off the 0.405 in (10.3 mm) outside diameter twice, once on each side, which leaves that bore and a flow area of 0.06 in².

How much does NPS 1/8 schedule 40 pipe weigh?

0.24 lb/ft (0.36 kg/m) as plain-end carbon steel, on a wall thickness of 0.068 in (1.7 mm). It holds 0.003 US gallons of water per foot.

What is the metric equivalent of NPS 1/8?

DN 6. DN is a dimensionless designator, not a measurement — DN 6 pipe does not measure 6 mm anywhere. Its actual outside diameter is 10.3 mm.
