What NPS 14 is

NPS 14 pipe measures 14.000 in (355.6 mm) across the outside, so here the size designation and the outside diameter in inches are the same number. That holds from NPS 14 upward and for no size below it. The metric designator is DN 350, which is likewise a label and not a measurement in millimetres. Whatever the schedule, the outside diameter stays at 14.000 in (355.6 mm): a heavier wall grows inward and takes the difference out of the bore.

The first size in which the NPS number and the outside diameter in inches are the same figure. Used for main headers, cooling water and flare lines.

Reading the table

B36.10 lists 13 designations in NPS 14, which between them give 12 different wall thicknesses. The lightest is Schedule 5 at 0.156 in (4.0 mm) and the heaviest is Schedule 160 at 1.406 in (35.7 mm). Going from one to the other takes the bore from 13.688 in (347.7 mm) down to 11.188 in (284.2 mm), which cuts the flow area by 33% and raises the weight from 23.09 to 189.29 lb/ft, a factor of 8.2.

Standard weight equals Schedule 30 in this size. Extra strong does not coincide with a numbered schedule here. Where two designations share a wall they are the same pipe under two names, and either may appear on a mill certificate. The match is specific to the size, so it is the wall thickness in inches that should go on a requisition, not the designation alone.

For handling and support loads, a 20 ft length of standard weight NPS 14 pipe weighs about 1092 lb (495 kg) empty and 2288 lb (1038 kg) full of water. The water-filled figure is the one that governs pipe support spacing and the load during a hydrostatic test, including on lines that will carry gas or vapour in service.

Flow and pressure capacity by schedule

Two questions decide a schedule: how much the bore will carry, and how much pressure the wall will hold. The flow columns give the water flow through each bore at three velocities that bracket ordinary liquid service, from a pump suction line at the low end to a pump discharge line at the high end. The pressure columns are the ASME B31.3 pressure design equation solved for pressure, first on the nominal wall and then on the wall after the 12.5% mill under-tolerance that seamless pipe is allowed.

Flanges for NPS 14 pipe

ASME B16.5 sizes a flange from the NPS and the pressure class, never from the schedule, so every flange below fits every wall in the table above. In NPS 14 a Class 150 flange is 21.00 in (533.4 mm) across and 1.31 in (33.3 mm) thick, held by 12 bolts of 1 in. A Class 1500 flange for the same pipe is 29.50 in (749.3 mm) across and 5.25 in (133.3 mm) thick, on 16 bolts of 2 1/4 in. Because the bolt circle moves with the class, flanges of two different classes will not bolt together even though both fit the pipe.

A weld neck flange for standard weight NPS 14 pipe is bored 13.250 in (336.5 mm); ordered for Schedule 160 it is bored 11.188 in (284.2 mm). The schedule therefore belongs on every weld neck flange requisition.

Buttweld fittings for NPS 14 pipe

ASME B16.9 sets the dimensions of a buttweld fitting from the NPS alone, so the figures below hold for every schedule in the size. A long radius 90° elbow in NPS 14 measures 21.00 in (533.4 mm) from its centre to each end, which is the figure a piping layout adds for every change of direction. The fitting is still ordered to a schedule, because its wall has to match the pipe at the weld.

Common questions

What is the outside diameter of NPS 14 pipe?

NPS 14 pipe has an outside diameter of 14.000 in (355.6 mm). That outside diameter is fixed: it is identical in every schedule, and only the wall thickness — and therefore the bore — changes from one schedule to the next.

What is the inside diameter of NPS 14 schedule 40 pipe?

13.124 in (333.3 mm). The 0.438 in (11.1 mm) wall comes off the 14.000 in (355.6 mm) outside diameter twice, once on each side, which leaves that bore and a flow area of 135.28 in².

How much does NPS 14 schedule 40 pipe weigh?

63.50 lb/ft (94.50 kg/m) as plain-end carbon steel, on a wall thickness of 0.438 in (11.1 mm). It holds 7.027 US gallons of water per foot.

What is the metric equivalent of NPS 14?

DN 350. DN is a dimensionless designator, not a measurement — DN 350 pipe does not measure 350 mm anywhere. Its actual outside diameter is 355.6 mm.
