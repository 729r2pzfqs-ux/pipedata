Across every size B36.19M publishes in 5S, the wall thickness is identical to B36.10M Schedule 5. The difference between them is the material, the permitted tolerances and the size range — not the wall.

Common questions

Is 5S the same as Schedule 5?

In wall thickness, yes. Every size B36.19M publishes in 5S carries the same wall as Schedule 5. The S suffix always signals stainless pipe to ASME B36.19M, which also stops at NPS 30 in this schedule where B36.10M continues.

What sizes does 5S come in?

NPS 1/2 through NPS 30 — 21 sizes. B36.19M is a shorter standard than B36.10M: it publishes 40S and 80S only through NPS 12, and 5S and 10S only through NPS 30. Beyond that, stainless pipe is ordered to a B36.10M schedule.

Why is 5S pipe used?

Stainless costs several times what carbon steel does, so stainless lines are run as thin as the pressure allows. The S-schedules exist to give that thin end of the range a designation — 10S in particular is the workhorse wall for low-pressure stainless process and sanitary piping.
