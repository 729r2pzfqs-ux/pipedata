Where a Class 2500 lap joint flange is used

Class 2500 is the heaviest class in ASME B16.5. It is used where pressure leaves no alternative, and at this class many designs avoid flanged joints altogether in favour of welded ones.

A lap joint flange is loose on the pipe and bears against a stub end. It is rarely chosen at this class, where the joint is expected to carry bending as well as pressure.

The number 2500 is a designation and not a pressure. A Class 2500 flange in A105 carbon steel is rated 6170 psig (425.4 bar) at 100 °F, 5280 psig at 400 °F, 4730 psig at 600 °F and 3430 psig at 800 °F. In Type 304 stainless, Group 2.1, it starts at 6000 psig and still holds 3380 psig at 800 °F, so the material and the metal temperature have to be fixed before the class can be.

Flange thickness excludes the raised face. Bolt diameter is nominal; stud bolts are normally ASTM A193 B7 with A194 2H nuts.

Across the 14 sizes published in Class 2500, the flange grows from 5.25 in (133.3 mm) across and 1.19 in (30.2 mm) thick at NPS 1/2 to 30.00 in (762.0 mm) across and 7.25 in (184.1 mm) thick at NPS 12. The bolt count goes 4 from NPS 1/2, 8 from NPS 2 and 12 from NPS 8, always in multiples of four so that the holes can straddle the centrelines. The largest bolt in the class is 2 3/4 in, first needed at NPS 12. Against Class 150, an NPS 6 flange in Class 2500 is 8.00 in larger in diameter and 4.5 times as thick.

Length through hub is not shown for the lap joint flange. ASME B16.5 tabulates it separately from the slip-on hub, and from NPS 14 upward the lap joint hub is the longer of the two. Those figures are not yet on this site.

ASME B16.5 publishes Class 2500 flanges in NPS 1/2 through NPS 12 only. Above NPS 12 there is no Class 2500 flange in the standard.

Bolt holes, spacing and torque

Bolt holes in ASME B16.5 flanges are drilled 1/8 in larger than the nominal bolt and are set to straddle the vertical and horizontal centrelines, never to sit on them. Spacing is the distance between neighbouring holes measured round the bolt circle, which shows how much room there is for a wrench or a tensioner. The torque column is a starting figure for a lubricated stud, not a joint-specific value.

Common questions

How many bolts does a Class 2500 lap joint flange use?

NPS 1/2 takes 4 × 3/4 in; NPS 3/4 takes 4 × 3/4 in; NPS 1 takes 4 × 7/8 in; NPS 1 1/4 takes 4 × 1 in; NPS 1 1/2 takes 4 × 1 1/8 in. Bolt count rises with size — see the table above for the full range.

What pressure is a Class 2500 flange rated for?

Class is a designation, not a pressure. A Class 2500 flange in A105 carbon steel is rated 6170 psig (425.4 bar) at 100 °F, and that rating falls as metal temperature rises. In stainless the same class starts lower at ambient but holds up far better in the heat — see the P-T rating tables.

Will a Class 2500 flange bolt to a Class 150 flange?

No. Bolt circle diameter, bolt count and bolt size all change with class, so flanges of different classes in the same NPS will not mate. The only exceptions inside B16.5 are the deliberate ones the standard calls out, such as Class 400 sharing Class 600 dimensions in NPS 1/2 through 3 1/2.
