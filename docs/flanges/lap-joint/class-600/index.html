Where a Class 600 lap joint flange is used

Class 600 is the first of the high-pressure classes in everyday use, common on high-pressure steam, boiler feed water and hydrocarbon process lines.

A lap joint flange is loose on the pipe and bears against a stub end. It is less common at this class, because the joint has little resistance to bending; where it is used, it is for alloy lines that are dismantled often.

The number 600 is a designation and not a pressure. A Class 600 flange in A105 carbon steel is rated 1480 psig (102.0 bar) at 100 °F, 1265 psig at 400 °F, 1135 psig at 600 °F and 825 psig at 800 °F. In Type 304 stainless, Group 2.1, it starts at 1440 psig and still holds 810 psig at 800 °F, so the material and the metal temperature have to be fixed before the class can be.

Flange thickness excludes the raised face. Bolt diameter is nominal; stud bolts are normally ASTM A193 B7 with A194 2H nuts.

Across the 20 sizes published in Class 600, the flange grows from 3.75 in (95.2 mm) across and 0.56 in (14.2 mm) thick at NPS 1/2 to 37.00 in (939.8 mm) across and 4.00 in (101.6 mm) thick at NPS 24. The bolt count goes 4 from NPS 1/2, 8 from NPS 2, 12 from NPS 6, 16 from NPS 10, 20 from NPS 12 and 24 from NPS 20, always in multiples of four so that the holes can straddle the centrelines. The largest bolt in the class is 1 7/8 in, first needed at NPS 24. Against Class 150, an NPS 6 flange in Class 600 is 3.00 in larger in diameter and 2.0 times as thick.

Bolt holes, spacing and torque

Bolt holes in ASME B16.5 flanges are drilled 1/8 in larger than the nominal bolt and are set to straddle the vertical and horizontal centrelines, never to sit on them. Spacing is the distance between neighbouring holes measured round the bolt circle, which shows how much room there is for a wrench or a tensioner. The torque column is a starting figure for a lubricated stud, not a joint-specific value.

Common questions

How many bolts does a Class 600 lap joint flange use?

NPS 1/2 takes 4 × 1/2 in; NPS 3/4 takes 4 × 5/8 in; NPS 1 takes 4 × 5/8 in; NPS 1 1/4 takes 4 × 5/8 in; NPS 1 1/2 takes 4 × 3/4 in. Bolt count rises with size — see the table above for the full range.

What pressure is a Class 600 flange rated for?

Class is a designation, not a pressure. A Class 600 flange in A105 carbon steel is rated 1480 psig (102.0 bar) at 100 °F, and that rating falls as metal temperature rises. In stainless the same class starts lower at ambient but holds up far better in the heat — see the P-T rating tables.

Will a Class 600 flange bolt to a Class 150 flange?

No. Bolt circle diameter, bolt count and bolt size all change with class, so flanges of different classes in the same NPS will not mate. The only exceptions inside B16.5 are the deliberate ones the standard calls out, such as Class 400 sharing Class 600 dimensions in NPS 1/2 through 3 1/2.
