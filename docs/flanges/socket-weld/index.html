Socket Weld Flange Dimensions

The pipe drops into a counterbored socket and is fillet-welded on the outside only. Published in NPS 1/2 through NPS 3 in Classes 150, 300 and 600 and through NPS 2 1/2 in Class 1500; the small-bore equivalent of a slip-on.

Strengths

  • Good static strength in small bore, and easy to fit up
  • No weld metal can protrude into the bore

Limitations

  • Needs a 1/16 in expansion gap at the socket bottom to avoid weld cracking
  • The gap is a crevice — poor choice for corrosive or sanitary service

Common questions

When should I use a socket weld flange?

Small-bore high-pressure lines: instrument, hydraulic and steam trace piping.

What pressure classes does a socket weld flange come in?

ASME B16.5 publishes the socket weld flange in Class 150, Class 300, Class 600 and Class 1500, in sizes NPS 1/2 through NPS 3 in Classes 150, 300 and 600 and NPS 2 1/2 in Class 1500. It does not publish a socket weld flange in Class 400, 900 or 2500. Above NPS 24 large-diameter flanges are covered by ASME B16.47 instead.

Does the flange face type change these dimensions?

The flange outside diameter, bolt circle and bolting are the same whatever the face. What changes is the facing itself and the flange thickness measurement: B16.5 thickness excludes the raised face, so a Class 300 raised-face flange is 0.06 in thicker overall than the tabulated figure. Ring-type joint flanges are grooved instead and are dimensioned separately.
